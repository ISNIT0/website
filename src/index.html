Forest City

A complete city for one million people, east of Cambridge.

I co-founded Forest City with Shiv Malik. It is not a housing estate — it is a whole city, planned properly from day one and built over 30 years on 45,000 acres straddling the Cambridgeshire–Suffolk border between Newmarket and Haverhill: 400,000 permanently affordable homes, a nature reserve twice the size of Epping Forest, a new reservoir and a new railway.

Against over £1 million for the equivalent in Cambridge. The mechanism is simple: you own the building, and the land stays with the community, held in trust so it cannot be flipped for profit.

Launched at The O2, October 2025

We announced Forest City on 23 October 2025 to a crowd of over 1,200 people at indigo at The O2 in London. A 295-page feasibility report, We Can Build a City, went to the Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government — written by more than 40 experts, all working pro bono.

Advisory board

Dame Patricia Hewitt, former Secretary of State for Trade & Industry, chairs the advisory board. She is joined by Professor Tim Leunig, former chief economic adviser to two prime ministers; Steve McAdam, masterplanner on the Olympics and King’s Cross; Jackie Sadek; and Paul Johnson, former head of the Institute for Fiscal Studies. Sir Tim Smit, founder of the Eden Project, has also come on board.

Looking for Growth

From a Camden bar to Downing Street.

I co-founded Looking for Growth in late 2024 with Lawrence Newport and James Newport. We launched with 400 people packed into a bar in Camden, and published our own infrastructure bill. The government subsequently began briefing parts of its own Planning Bill early, with elements moving closer to the proposals we had written.

We are a cross-party group of entrepreneurs who tend to feel left out of politics — anti-partisan, pro-growth, and impatient with decline. In October 2025 we held “Make or Break” at indigo at The O2, with speakers including Dominic Cummings, Matt Clifford and Chris Curtis.

Next up: the Optimistic Picnic in Hyde Park — hundreds of people who think things can actually get better, no agenda, no speeches.

Cleaning the Tube

Trains on the Bakerloo line had been left covered in graffiti for months. I led a team of volunteers, including the GB News host Tom Harwood, to clean them ourselves. The London Standard broke the story; the Sunday Times took it up from there.

ElevenLabs

Talk to a statue.

I work on Growth at ElevenLabs. The project I am most asked about is Talk to a Statue: photograph a statue, and the app identifies the figures, then lets you hold a real-time voice conversation with them — each character in a distinct, period-appropriate voice. I built it with Voice Design and the Agent APIs, one-shot in Cursor from an empty Next.js project.

A museum chief executive tracked down my WhatsApp number to ask how I had built it, after a team of ten had spent a year on the same problem. I later gave the talk at AI Engineer London.

I also run ElevenHacks, and I organised a two-day hackathon with ElevenLabs and the UK Government’s Incubator for AI — builders sitting with the people actually deploying AI across Whitehall. The Financial Times has written about the company.

Amplitude — Engineering Manager, then Head of AI

I shipped Ask Amplitude, which lets people state a query in natural language instead of constructing it by hand — framed deliberately as a coach rather than a black box. I recorded a live Latent Space episode with Jeffrey Wang, Amplitude’s co-founder and chief architect, at an AI + Product hackathon where 150+ hackers competed for over $22,500 in prizes. The argument I wanted to make: AI may need analytics more than analytics needs AI, and first-party behavioural data is the moat. That is where RLHB — reinforcement learning from human behaviour came from.

Thanks to AI, a software engineer can now be an artist.

Future House — Founder

I founded Future House at 27, in an old coaching house in Hackney: London’s techno-optimist members’ club, with members spanning AI, robotics, space and policy, on the premise that technology is humanity’s leverage. It ran for six months and then I closed it. It is here precisely because it closed — shipping things means sometimes killing them.

Coronavirus Tech Handbook — CTO

Google Docs hits a soft limit at around 100 concurrent editors, and operational transform was not going to scale to what the project needed — so I built a CRDT-based real-time collaborative editing platform instead, used by thousands of contributors. Developed at Newspeak House, it became arguably the world’s largest open-source library of coronavirus tools and data: over 500 pages, viewed more than 500,000 times.
